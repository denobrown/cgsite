Every cloud engagement with a CBK-supervised client eventually reaches the same question: where does the data actually live, physically, and can we prove it. "It's in the cloud" is not an answer the Central Bank of Kenya's cloud computing guidance accepts, and it shouldn't be one your architecture accepts either.

## The guidance is about evidence, not intent

CBK's expectation isn't that you avoid public cloud — most of our regulated clients run substantial hybrid AWS or Azure workloads, and that's appropriate. The expectation is that you can produce, on request, a current and accurate map of which physical region holds which data class, including backups and DR replicas. Intent to comply is not evidence of compliance.

We've inherited more than one client architecture where the primary database was correctly documented as residing in a specific AWS region, but automated cross-region backup replication — enabled by a well-meaning infrastructure engineer for redundancy — had quietly been copying encrypted snapshots to a second region nobody had documented or approved for that data class.

## Why Raxio, specifically

CloudGrid Africa is co-located at Raxio Data Centre Nairobi, a Tier III facility, for the workloads and clients where in-country residency is a hard requirement rather than a preference. Tier III gives us concurrently maintainable infrastructure — redundant power and cooling paths that can undergo planned maintenance without a service interruption — at a facility whose physical location and jurisdiction is unambiguous and auditable.

This isn't a rejection of public cloud. It's a second, deliberate tier in the architecture: workloads that must demonstrably never leave Kenyan jurisdiction sit at Raxio; workloads with more flexible residency requirements run hybrid across AWS and Azure with documented data-flow maps. The decision of which workload goes where is made once, explicitly, at design time — not discovered during an audit.

## The practical test we run with clients

Before any new workload goes live, we ask the client to answer one question in writing: if the ODPC or CBK asked for a current data-residency map tomorrow, could you produce one without an engineering investigation first? If the honest answer is no, that's the first thing we fix — usually before touching a single line of application code.
